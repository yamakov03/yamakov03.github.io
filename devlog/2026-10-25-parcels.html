Parcels

Oct 25, 2026

A city builder in the works. The goal is realism, 60 FPS, and support for Metro-level planning.

There's a problem with today's city builders. They are tough to scale, have unrealistic economies, and allow you to build up land and have infinite money. I wanted to start from scratch, taking the learnings from some of the most popular city builders out there, and create the city builder of my dreams.

This is my first devlog for this project I've been working on for the past few days. I am building it in Rust and am using wgpu for rendering and winit + egui for the game's interface. So far, I have working zoning, density modeling, roads, utilities, traffic AI, transit (buses for now), and a pixel-style mesh system that is reminiscent of modern American cities.

The goal here is not to build reality, but to get as close to it as possible, and make it fun to play, too! I aspire this game to achieve a minimum of 60 frames per second while also supporting populations upwards of 100k. I also want to add fine-tuned control over manufacturing and supply chain systems end-to-end, as well as realistic transit systems. Here's what it looks like so far:

What's next

This game will likely go through more iterations as I improve each individual system, and how they play with each other. Long-term, I plan to swap out the visuals for a real game engine like Unreal Engine or Bevy Engine, which is Rust-native. I don't want to get bogged down with these engines early on, so building out the sim core (which would just ship as a .dll with the executable later) standalone has been a great way to iterate.

Thanks for reading! I'll keep making these to document the game through its development.
