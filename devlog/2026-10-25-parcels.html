Parcels

Oct 25, 2026

I'm making a city builder. Cities: Skylines is the one I keep arguing with. Demand that fights the city you actually wanted, traffic that ignores lanes, goods that never show up, and the sim giving up once the city gets big. I want 60 fps, and I want a city that can get as big as Tokyo. That's the goal, anyway.

A parcel is just a lot. Somebody owns it, zoning says what's allowed, there's a building, and it faces a road. People live there, jobs are there, trucks show up, pipes run to it. When a number pops up in the panel, I want to click it and land on the household, the lot, or the money behind it.

The title bar still says Ground Truth. That was the name in my notes. I've been calling it Parcels, and the window hasn't gotten the memo.

Right now I can paint lots, draw roads, zone them, and let the day run. Power, water, sewage, schools, clinics, a developer that builds on empty lots, and buses are all in. The city in these shots is tiny, and it's happily sitting at 60 fps. I want the same thing when it's Tokyo-sized.

Lots

Parcel 8 is a shop downtown. 1,690 m², zoned High, and it's already as tall as the zone will let it get. 12 of 24 jobs are filled, and there's a stock of 8 on the shelf. The panel is a little annoyed about the empty jobs, and it also tells me the building is stuck because High is the cap. Road and power are both live, so the lot is allowed to be here.

Zoning is the rulebook for a lot. I paint homes, shops, industry, or mixed, and if I drag into the same zone next door they merge. A lot can build once it touches a road and has power. Green means you're good, amber means I only grabbed part of the lot, red means something's in the way. This map has 8 lots that can build. Four of them are low density. Nobody has gone mid or high yet. Give it a minute.

Empty lots fill in when a developer looks at them. It checks households, rent, and whether a shop would actually survive, and then maybe it builds. Parcel 2 is homes: 1,455 m², 3 households, density Low, with room in the zone to go all the way to High. Stepping up to Medium takes four checks, and this lot passed all of them. Power, water, and sewage are on. A school and a clinic can reach it and still have a free seat. At least half the nearby jobs are filled. Next time the developer comes around, this one should grow. The issue list is empty, which I'll take. The clock says day 1, morning commute.

Traffic

The blue lines are trips. Where someone starts, and where they need to end up. Under that, roads are lanes, and you can only turn where the lanes let you. I can make a street one-way or ban a turn, and the paths have to deal with it. If a road is packed, driving costs more time, and the next person might walk or hop on the bus.

The road I clicked here had 0 walkers and 44 drivers. The legend calls roads free, busy, or jammed. This one is having a morning.

Utilities

Power and water are actual networks. Sewage is the third one. A plant has a capacity, and some of it leaks off along the way, so a lot can go dark while the plant is running fine. Clinic 2 has 120 seats, zero patients, a 200 m radius, and 7 parcels inside it. Very prepared for day 1. A homes lot won't level up until a clinic and a school can actually take somebody.

Buses

Buses drive on the roads, same as everyone else. A line is a bunch of stops stuck to the curb, and the bus follows the streets between them. The cyan one is stops 1 through 8. From above you get the stop numbers, which way they're headed, and a second color where another route shares the street. Buses have a capacity. Walk, drive, and bus get compared on the whole trip, and if the bus is crawling, people skip it.

The sim

The sim is Rust. That's the part I actually trust. Households, firms, shipments, lanes, utilities, buses, and the money all live there. Same seed and same inputs, and I get the same city back. The window tells it what I did, like placing a road, zoning a lot, or following some commuter, and then reads back what happened.

The window is a workbench. winit, wgpu for the roads and buildings, egui for the panels. The buildings are boxes with little lit windows. I like them. Unreal is the nicer client I want later, hooked up to this same sim. The tests run with no window, which is great, because I can't debug a big city by squinting at it.

Far from the camera, people are counts and trips. Up close, or if I click one, they're a person headed to work, school, a shop, or home. Jobs, goods, and money stay tracked the whole time. On a Tokyo-sized city, most of it would stay as counts. The block I'm looking at is the one that gets drawn person by person. That's how I'm hoping to keep 60 fps.

Look at it with cargo run -p groundtruth-lab. Check it with cargo test -p groundtruth-sim.

So far

One-ways and turn bans work. The budget can show income and spending. Shops get restocked by truck. A real tax setup, rail, ships, freight contracts, and the Unreal client are still on the list.

The bar for this version is pretty ordinary. Follow 10 people and watch all 10 actually arrive. Sell something in a shop and trace it back to a delivery. Ban a turn and watch the traffic go around. If the city is broke, I want to see why in about a minute.
